Meteora and Kalabaka travel guide

Meteora means "suspended in the air": sandstone pillars up to 400 metres high with monasteries on top. Six are still active and open to visitors.

How to get there

Kalabaka and Kastraki are the twin gateways to Meteora. Hellenic Train connects Athens directly to Kalabaka in about 4 hours. A scenic 14 km paved ring road loops around all six active monasteries, and local municipal buses leave Kalabaka bus station for the monasteries three times a day from April to October.

The six active monasteries

  • Great Meteoron: the oldest (1356), highest (613 m) and largest, with old wine cellars, refectories and an ossuary.
  • Varlaam: second largest, known for 16th-century Byzantine frescoes and the original wooden rope-and-net hoist tower.
  • Roussanou (St. Barbara): an elegant clifftop nunnery reached by wooden bridges over a canyon.
  • Holy Trinity (Agia Triada): the most isolated monastery, reached by 140 rock-hewn steps and seen in the James Bond film For Your Eyes Only.
  • Agios Stefanos and Agios Nikolaos Anapafsas: the other two visitable monasteries, with Anapafsas known for frescoes painted in 1527.

Plan around the closing days

Each active monastery closes on a different day of the week. For example, Great Meteoron is closed on Tuesdays and Varlaam on Fridays. Check the current schedule before you set your route so you never arrive at closed gates.

Dress code

Men need long trousers, with no shorts or sleeveless shirts. Women are expected to wear a long skirt covering the knees, and wraparound skirts are usually loaned at the entrance.

Sunset at Psaropetra

Arrive at the Psaropetra viewpoint about 45 minutes before sunset. As the sun drops behind the Pindus mountains, the sandstone pillars glow amber and purple.

Practical tips

  • Monastery tickets are cash only, and there are no ATMs up on the rocks. Use the ATMs in Kalabaka or Kastraki first.
  • You can walk from Kastraki up to the monasteries along the old monk footpaths. Wear hiking boots with good ankle support and carry at least 1.5 litres of water.
  • Kastraki sits right under the pillars and has family-run tavernas, and Theopetra Prehistoric Cave is another stop away from the monasteries.

Always check current ferry, bus and opening-time schedules before you book: they change with the season.
