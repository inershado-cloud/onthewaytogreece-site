Santorini travel guide

Santorini is what is left of a volcano that erupted around 1600 BC: a crescent of cliff about 300 metres above a flooded caldera. Here is what to know before you go.

When to go

The best months are May to October. Cruise ships land thousands of day-trippers around midday, so mornings and evenings are far calmer. August beds, flights and ferries fill months ahead.

How to get there

  • By ferry: boats from Piraeus and Rafina, and from the rest of the Cyclades, arrive at Athinios on the west coast. High-speed boats from Piraeus take around 5 hours; conventional ferries roughly 6 to 11. Athinios sits about 8 km below Fira and 20 km from Oia, and the road up is a steep switchback, so arrange a transfer before you land.
  • By air: Santorini airport (JTR) has flights from Athens year-round and from much of Europe through the summer. It is about 6 km from Fira, near Kamari on the east coast.

Where things are

Whitewashed Fira, Firostefani and Imerovigli run along the caldera rim and Oia holds the northern tip. The far side of the island slopes down to long black-sand beaches at Kamari and Perissa. Thirasia lies opposite and the black cone of Nea Kameni sits in the middle of the caldera.

Famous for

  • the caldera left by the Bronze Age eruption
  • Oia's blue domes and its sunset
  • Akrotiri, the Minoan town buried in ash
  • black-sand beaches at Kamari and Perissa
  • Assyrtiko wine and sweet Vinsanto
  • the Red Beach under its volcanic cliff

Practical tips

  • The Fira to Oia caldera path is about 10 km with almost no shade, so start early.
  • Black sand gets fiercely hot by midday. Take sandals, not bare feet.
  • The Red Beach sits under an unstable cliff and access is sometimes restricted. Check locally before you go.

Always check current ferry, bus and opening-time schedules before you book: they change with the season.
