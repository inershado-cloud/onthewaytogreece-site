Naxos travel guide

Naxos is the largest and greenest island of the Cyclades: mountains, marble villages and long pale beaches. Here is how to plan it.

When to go

The best months are May to October. The meltemi wind blows hardest in July and August, mostly on the exposed west and south coasts. Ferries and rooms fill fast in August, so book crossings and accommodation well ahead. Bus timetables thin out right off-season.

How to get there

  • By ferry: boats link Naxos with Piraeus, Rafina and Lavrio, and with Paros, Mykonos, Santorini, Ios, Syros and the Small Cyclades. From Piraeus, fast catamarans take about 3¼ hours and conventional ferries roughly 5 to 5½. Everything arrives at the single port on the edge of Chora, so most town hotels are a short walk away.
  • By air: Naxos airport (JNX) has small turboprops from Athens with Olympic Air and Sky Express. The runway is about 3 km south of Chora, just behind Agios Prokopios beach. Seats are few, so summer flights sell out early.

Getting around

Naxos is big. Hire a car or scooter to reach the mountain villages and the southern beaches. Buses link Chora with the main beaches and villages.

Beaches

  • Agios Georgios: shallow sandy town beach within walking distance of Chora, safe for children.
  • Agios Prokopios: long golden strand with beach bars and calm clear water, one of the busiest.
  • Agia Anna: small harbour, tavernas and easy swimming just south of Agios Prokopios.
  • Plaka: kilometres of soft white sand behind low dunes, still relaxed and low-rise.
  • Orkos: small sandy coves between Plaka and Mikri Vigla, few facilities.
  • Mikri Vigla: twin bays split by a rocky headland, the windsurfing and kitesurfing hub.
  • Alyko: dune beach backed by a cedar forest, with quiet coves.

Beyond the beach

  • The Portara: the marble gateway of an unfinished temple of Apollo on the Palatia islet.
  • The Kastro: the Venetian castle quarter above Chora, with towers, lanes and museums.
  • Mount Zas: just over 1,000 m, the highest peak in the Cyclades. Take sturdy shoes, a hat and plenty of water, as there is almost no shade.
  • Halki and Apeiranthos: marble-paved villages; Halki is home to the Vallindras kitron distillery of 1896.
  • Temple of Demeter: an Archaic marble sanctuary of around 530 BC near Sangri.
  • The kouros statues: unfinished giant Archaic figures at Flerio, Melanes and Apollonas.
  • Small Cyclades trip: boats to Koufonisia, Iraklia, Schinoussa and Donousa leave from Naxos port.

What to eat

Try graviera Naxou (the island's PDO cheese), arseniko (a hard, sharp sheep and goat cheese), Naxos potatoes (PGI), rosto (pork slow-cooked in wine, tomato and garlic) and kitron, the citron-leaf liqueur from Halki.

Always check current ferry, bus and opening-time schedules before you book: they change with the season.
